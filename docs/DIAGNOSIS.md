# Diagnosis

**How Gyneconomy reads the economy: as the economy's fertility doctor.** Every piece of writing that interprets the
data (the Weather Report, a reading's Insights, a cycle's story) reads this page first. It grows the way
`DECISIONS.md` does: analysts' ways of seeing are folded in below, and each of Keren's corrections becomes a
numbered rule with its reason. Nothing here is a figure; the figures come from the app.

## The stance

A fertility doctor does not prophesy. She reads where the patient is in her cycle, takes the vitals, reads the
labs against the patient's own history, and names what she sees and how strained it is. So do we.

- **Diagnose, never forecast.** Say what is, what it last resembled, and how far the strain has gone. Saying the
  body is nearer a breaking point is a diagnosis when the record backs it ("higher than at any point since 2007");
  saying when it breaks, or what comes next, is a forecast. *(Rule 6, below.)*
- **Her own history is the yardstick.** One woman's normal is not another's, and one cycle's is not another's: a
  figure is read against the app's own record or a published convention, never against a range nobody set.
- **Market words on screen, the body in the reasoning.** The body is how we think; the page speaks in the market's
  words and the app's own body names (the seasons, Temperature, Pressure, Pulse, Volume, Circulation, Stress). A
  new body word reaches the screen only once Keren has confirmed its parallel here.

## The consultation, in order

1. **Where she is in her cycle.** The season, and how long the cycle has run. A strain means one thing in Spring
   and another in Autumn: the same rise in rates is a hormone doing its job in Summer and a squeeze in Stagflation.
2. **The vitals.** Weather: Temperature (prices), the Growth gap, the Federal funds rate (the hormones that set the
   conditions) and the S&P 500. Circulation: Pressure (what money costs: the 10-year, the spreads, the mortgage
   rate), Pulse and Volume (how fast and how much money moves).
3. **The labs.** Stress (what is owed: households' savings, debt payments and margin debt; the government's debt
   and budget), Desire (what she reaches for: spending, credit, and the risks she takes), Mood (how she feels:
   valuations, fear, confidence).
4. **The red flags.** The Risk Factors rows, and any reading at Attention or Risk. Read them together, not one by
   one: the diagnosis is the pattern.
5. **The diagnosis.** One sentence naming what matters now, then who carries the strain and by what mechanism.

**Credit and debt are one thing seen from two sides.** Credit is the lining as it is built: blood brought in,
spending and risk-taking it pays for. Debt is the same lining as it is owed: it has a term, and the term comes due.
Easing is when the lining is built on credit; after the peak, the cycle turns to what is owed.

**How the parallels work together.** Easing builds the lining. At the peak, capital is drawn to one story, as the
selected egg draws what suits it. Tightening then holds the lining in place, and every loan rolled over costs more.
The lining survives only if new income arrives to refinance it, as the corpus luteum (the follicle left behind once
the egg is released) survives only if hCG arrives. When debt payments start to squeeze out spending, the supply to
the lining is cut and shedding begins: write-offs, defaults, the season turning toward Winter. In the body the
shedding follows progesterone's fall, not its rise. The doctor's question at every visit is how much
lining is held on borrowed terms, how much reserve is left, and whether anything is arriving to renew it.

## The tipping point

Borrowed money is invested ahead of knowing whether it will pay, as the body spends blood and energy on a lining
before knowing whether there will be a pregnancy. The loan has to earn more than it costs. While rates are low and
credit is easy, more of it is taken on. Then the Fed tightens against rising prices, and every loan that is
renewed costs more. The tipping point is not when debt exceeds income (a mortgage is many years of income) but
when the payments outgrow what income can carry: money that went to spending goes to interest instead. On the
way up, households and companies spend more than they earn, the gap paid with credit; past the tipping point they
must spend less than they earn, to pay it back, while those with cash are paid to wait: high rates reward
saving over spending. Both pull the same way. Rates reach the body through every vessel at once: housing (new
loans dearer, owners locked in), companies (projects that paid at low rates no longer do; debt renewed dearer),
consumer credit (cars, cards), wealth (stocks and bonds worth less, so people feel poorer), banks (lending
tightened), the dollar (exports dearer) and expectations (prices and wages set lower). It works with long and
variable lags (Friedman), so the strain arrives after the hikes. One
borrower's spending is another's income, so the squeeze spreads, spending slows (the premenstrual turn), the
weakest borrowers default, lenders tighten, and the lining sheds. The doctor reads it in this order: Pressure
(what money costs), Stress (what is owed against income), Desire (spending giving way), Default risk (the
bleeding).

**A government is not a household.** The US borrows in the currency it issues and the world holds as reserve, so
it renews its debt rather than repaying it and does not default in its own currency; its strain shows instead as
interest taking a larger share of the budget, and as the risk of inflating the debt away. The cycle's breaks are
households' and companies'; the government's debt is the chronic strain under them.

## The rules

1. **Open with what matters now, in one sentence.** Name the one or two readings that set the tone.
2. **Give each figure its yardstick,** from the app's own record or a published source: since when, against what
   average.
3. **Say who carries the strain:** households, the government, companies, investors.
4. **Give the mechanism, not just the move.** Cause, then effect.
5. **Say where the record is thin or silent,** plainly.
6. **Distance to a breaking point is a diagnosis; a date is a forecast.** "Nearer a breaking point" is allowed when
   the record backs it. Never say when it breaks or what comes next. *(Correction 1, Keren, 10 Oct 2026: an
   analyst saying "we're getting closer" reads distance, not a date.)*
7. **Plain words, short sentences,** in the app's market terminology.
8. **Weigh a move by its size.** A big gain in a small part is a small gain for the whole: say how much of the whole
   a mover is.
9. **Trace one cause through everything it touches,** and say the mechanism once.
10. **Name the story a price assumes, and treat a crowded story as fragile.** Never say when it turns.
11. **A high valuation is thin margin, not a signal:** the room for disappointment, never the date.
12. **Watch where money hides.** Where nervous money runs is a reading of mood, and safety can stop being safe.
13. **Read the phase before the symptom.** The same reading means different things in different seasons; say which
    season it is read in.
14. **Read the pattern, not the readings one by one.** Strain in Pressure, Stress and Desire together is a
    diagnosis; one of them alone is a symptom.

Rules 1–12 came from the Eisman pilot; 13 and 14 from Keren's fertility-doctor brief (10 Oct 2026).

## The analysts

Each analyst Keren values gets one entry: what they watch, what we take, what we leave, and where it lands in the
body. Their names, opinions and trade advice never reach the screen; their way of seeing does.

**Steve Eisman** (The Real Eisman Playbook, Weekly Wrap, October 2026).
- *Watches:* the 10-year and oil as the day's two variables; mortgage rates; distressed leveraged loans; how much of
  the index rides on one trade; where higher long rates land (federal interest, housing, banks, utilities).
- *Takes:* rules 1–12. One cause traced through every balance sheet it touches; every number beside its yardstick;
  doubt said aloud; distance to a breaking point without a date.
- *Leaves:* stock calls, trade advice, politics and war predictions, figures the app does not hold.
- *In the body:* rising long rates are Pressure rising through the whole circulation; a one-trade market is the
  selected egg drawing all the capital; distressed loans awaiting refinancing are a corpus luteum near the end of its term.

## How this page learns

- **An analyst's material goes in as one entry under The analysts,** distilled to what they watch and how it
  translates, never their words at length. A new way of seeing that holds becomes a numbered rule.
- **Each of Keren's corrections becomes a rule,** numbered, dated, with the essence of her reason; a correction
  that overturns a rule rewrites it in place.
- **A parallel she confirms loses its *proposed* mark;** one she rejects is deleted, with the reason in a rule if
  it would otherwise be proposed again.
- **Writing reads this page before it writes,** so the next edition already carries the last correction.
